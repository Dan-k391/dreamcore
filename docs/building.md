# Building reference

## Code layout

- `js/core.js`: renderer, scene, camera, constants, the zone registry (`defZone`).
- `js/tex.js`: procedural textures. `js/mat.js`: materials (see [materials.md](materials.md)).
- `js/build.js`: the level builder and the registries places hand things to the rest of the game through.
- `js/props/<group>.js`: the prop kits (lights, metalwork, garden, play, structure); places call them for objects (see [props.md](props.md)).
- `js/zones/<place>.js`: everything about one place: its zones, materials, geometry, floors, lights, props. `js/level.js` imports them in order.
- `js/sky.js`, `water.js`, `lights.js`, `post.js`, `audio.js`, `player.js`, `physics.js`, `dev.js`, `main.js`.
- Imports only point back to earlier files, and places never import each other.
- `vendor/three/`: the three.js r170 files the game imports (the import map in `index.html` points there; no CDN). After changing any code, run `node tools/bundle.mjs` to rebuild `dreamcore.html`, the single-file copy; `tools/test.mjs` fails while it is stale. Imports must stay plain strings (`import('./x.js')`, not a computed path) and modules must not use `import.meta`; the bundler refuses both.

## Adding a place

A new file in `js/zones/` and one import line in `js/level.js`, plus these shared edits when they apply:

- A far-off place reached by a dark passage adds a row to `tunnels` in `js/zones/passages.js`.
- A new sky adds an entry to `skies` in `js/sky.js` and a key to `skyOnly` in `js/build.js`.
- A place that looks through a doorway at another place's pool adds its zone id to that pool's seen-from list in the other place's file; otherwise the water is hidden.
- Pools whose rectangles touch along an edge (the arcade channel and the night hall) are one sheet of water in `water.js` (`sheets`): one wave field, CPU wave copy, height map and plan over the box round them, so ripples, light and floating things carry across the join. Up to 4 pools to a sheet; inside the box, ground outside every pool is a wall to the waves. Join pools by making their rectangles share an edge, not by leaving a gap.
- Then run `node tools/pvs.mjs --port <port>`: it rewrites `js/seen.js`, which zones can be seen from which. Each place's meshes are drawn only from zones that can see it; a new zone is drawn from everywhere until it is listed, and a new doorway or window into an old zone needs the run too (`tools/test.mjs` checks it). Besides its 1 m grid it samples the walk list of `tools/walk.mjs` (every opening crossed at 10 cm, every zone's centre line, standing and mid-jump eyes), so narrow sight lines through a doorway are not stepped over; `tools/seams.mjs --pop` checks the same points.
- A new enclosed zone gives its reflection box in its zone definition, `env: [[minX, floorY, minZ], [maxX, ceilY, maxZ]]` (as `js/zones/mall.js` does), so reflections are box-projected to its walls; outdoor zones give none.
- A new place names its music: `music: '<piece>'` in its zone definition, a piece from `pieces` in `js/music.js` (a new piece keeps to the D major notes and the 10 s bar, and is balanced with `node tools/music.mjs`), or `music: null` for none.
- A new place's sound: its floors are heard from their materials (see Sound below); `step` names what the feet hear where no library material is under them, and `amb` adds what the place's air sounds like.

## Geometry

Shapes go into one list per material and are merged into one mesh each (`add(mat, geometry)`), so use few materials and get few draw calls. `add()` gives box-projected UVs in metres; `add(mat, geometry, true)` keeps the geometry's own UVs (for materials with a direction: brushed metal, wood, cloth).

Rounded boxes: import `RoundedBoxGeometry` from `build.js`, not from three's addons. It is the same shape, built once per set of arguments and handed out as a copy (the places ask for about 3800 of some 550 kinds, and building each one was a fifth of the load).

No two faces in one plane: one surface per plane (trim the hidden face, let one place own a shared face, a twin passage takes `passage()`'s wall depth so the doorway's reveal is the place's); trim, inlays and linings that sit proud stand at least 1.5 mm proud. The suite checks it (`node tools/seams.mjs --scan`).

## Making things solid

Used by `js/player.js` and `js/physics.js`. A solid blocks the body where it overlaps it above step height (0.35 m), its top is ground you can stand on, and its underside stops a jump. A jump peaks at about 0.84 m (rising against 16 m/s², falling at 24) and carries about 1.7 m walking (2.85 m/s), 3.5 m running (6 m/s) and 4.2 m at the 7.2 m/s a chain of strafing hops can build; at every landing height that is no farther than a 6 m/s run carried under the old single 11 m/s² arc (4.5 m on the level), so the level needs no new seals.

- `box()` is solid over its true extent by itself. `block()` is the same shape and never solid.
- For anything built otherwise, add its solid in the same place file:
  - `solid(x1, y1, z1, x2, y2, z2)`: an upright box.
  - `round(x, z, r, y1, y2)`: a post, trunk or column.
  - `turned(cx, cz, hx, hz, ry, y1, y2)`: a box turned by `rotateY(ry)`.
- A slope is a few stepped solids, or a height function in `flats` (`[x1, z1, x2, z2, (x, z) => height]`; a later entry overrides an earlier one).
- `slab(..., through = true)` gives the piers between its arches solids, plus stepped solids under each arch head. `post()` and `doorway()` add their own.
- If something looks like you could stand on it, you can; if a ledge leads out of the level, close it with an unseen thin solid.

## Loose things

`js/physics.js` moves them; fixed 60 Hz steps, deterministic under `dev.hold` + `dev.step`.

- A place pushes `{ mesh, r, pos, region, bounce, grip, ... }` onto `loose` in `js/build.js` (the fields are listed there): balls that fall, roll, float (`float`), stop the body (`solid`) and sleep when still. An `InstancedMesh` holds many.
- A swim ring is a torus mesh pushed onto `rings`; it floats on the pool's waves.
- Their shadows are redrawn on their own, over a cached copy of each map's static casters (see Light), so a moving ring or ball costs only itself.
- Budget, checked in `tools/test.mjs`: under 0.3 ms a frame at rest; in a splash under 1 ms median and under 3 ms at the 95th percentile.

## Light

- Nine shadow-casting lamps are shared by every fixture in the level and follow the player; a place registers fixtures with `lamp()` or `lit()` and they cost nothing more.
- What a lit pixel pays (scene pass alone at 2560x1440 High, measured in one page load with each term switched off in turn): the nine lamps 0.4-1.45 ms of 1.1-3.6 ms, their shadow lookups 0.25-1 of that (16 taps on RGBA-packed depth, weighted by a cubic B-spline in `core.js` instead of three's 3-texel box, which blurred edges and drew the texel grid as stair steps; one tap instead saves 0.1-0.6); the zone's fixtures up to 1.7 ms where there are many (gallery pool; 0.7 night hall, near 0 elsewhere), their distance-map lookups 0.64 and their lighting 0.32 of that; sun, ambient grid, reflection, macro field, triplanar, normal mapping and fog 0.3 ms or less each. `core.js` looks a lamp's or the sun's shadow up only where that light reaches the point (cone and reach) and the surface faces it, in a real branch (taps at lod 0: the maps have no mips); RE_Direct adds nothing elsewhere, so the picture is the same.
- Shadow maps are drawn only when asked. `shadow.needsUpdate` (a lamp moved to a new fixture, the sky changed, anything static changed) redraws a map in full and keeps (lamps only) a copy of it without the loose things. A lamp's map: a loose thing that moves asks with `shadow.loose` instead (`reshade` in `js/physics.js`): the copy is put back over the box where loose things were and are, and only they are drawn, the same map to the bit (checked in `tools/test.mjs`). The sun's map (4096 x 8192: its rows run along the sun, where the low sun stretches a texel 2.3 times on the floor, so the floor's texels are square, about 0.7 cm) has no copy (it would be another 256 MB): a loose thing under it asks with `shadow.loose` too, and the map is redrawn whole but only in the box where loose things were and are, its camera narrowed to that box so three culls the rest (0.25 ms at 1440p in the pool hall, the five lamps' copies about as much again). A ring or ball asks once it has moved 2 mm since its shadow was drawn, a third of a sun texel on the floor (4 cm made the shadow lag the ring and jump after it; asking every step made a drifting ring, 0.4 mm a step for ever, cost a redraw every frame). The lamps' copies cost 72 MB. Something new that moves and casts a shadow goes on physics.js's loose list or asks with `needsUpdate`.
- Each zone's ambient light is baked from a probe at load; a material gets it by being registered with the zone (`M(zone, ...)` or `P(zone, ...)`).
- *Fixtures* (`fixture(z, x, y, zc, power)` in `build.js`, called by `sconce`, `stepLight` and `poolLight` with a `power` in pool places, up to 24 a zone): cheap lights without a lamp of their own. Every material registered with the zone computes them (`mat.js` fixPars / fixMain, inside `envBox`), tiles and props alike, so a bench, rail or ball in a pool of light is lit by it. Each is shadowed: at load `lights.js` bakeFixtures renders, from every fixture, the distance to the nearest surface in every direction (what casts the lamps' shadows, less loose things and glass) into one octahedral atlas, 128 texels a fixture (`fixRT`, one texture unit; about 0.23 s for the 59 fixtures). Loose things are lit by fixtures but cast no fixture shadow. A fixture fainter than 0.01 at a point skips its shadow lookup.
- *Ambient that varies with place* (bounced, coloured light): each indoor zone (one with an `env` box) has a grid of 0.5 m cells over that box plus 1 m (`lights.js` ambientGrid / agWork / agPalette), stored in the fixture atlas below the fixtures' maps (RGBA half float, filtered; no extra texture unit): six tiles a layer (+x -x +y -y +z -z), rgb the light a surface facing that way receives, alpha 1 in an open cell and 0 (rgb 0) in a solid one. The bake: what is solid comes from the level's `colliders` (walls, floors, ceilings, seats, tables, columns; not drawn triangles); what each solid cell face is made of comes from the drawn triangles of every lit or glowing material, sampled about every 20 cm (the biggest triangle there wins; glow adds up by area); a material's colour is its library set's baked albedo at its smallest mip times its `color` (one draw a set, read back once, as soon as the library is baked). Every solid face onto an open cell is a patch; workers (up to four, grids shared biggest first) follow 24 rays from each patch to what they meet within 8 m, sum the direct light each takes from the zone's lamps (spot cone, reach) and fixtures (points and lines) shadowed through the solids, solve the bounces (each patch: its colour x what reaches it, plus its glow; 8 Jacobi rounds; a ray that meets nothing near sees the room's mean), then every open cell within two of a solid gathers over 48 rays what the patches send each of its six directions (and how open that way is within 2 m, as before), smoothed over its open neighbours (3 x 3 x 3, walls left out). Stored as a share of the room's mean (each channel: the probe's capture carries the room's level and overall colour), brightness softened to the square root, held to 0.4..1.6, scaled so the room's mean is what the old nearness-to-lamps term gave (x 0.9, matched on screen), half its colour kept, faded to plain within 4 m of an opening so neighbouring rooms meet as before. `mat.js` agLight reads it 0.3 m off the surface along its normal: two taps (a y pair) per axis the normal leans along (2 for a wall or floor, 4 for a column's side, at most 6), weighted by openness and renormalised, so light does not cross a wall. A solid cell lends, at 1/32 weight, the light of the open cell its face looks into (else the mean of its neighbours but the one behind, two rounds), so it counts only where every tap is solid: colliders mark every cell they overlap, so within about 0.5 m of a second surface (under the ceiling, in a corner, beside a column) the 0.3 m sample point sees only solid cells, and the lookup used to fall back to plain 1 there, a bright frame round every wall whose corners baked darker (and a pale square on the ceiling round a column's top); it scales only the ambient diffuse (reflections untouched); `light.bounce` (docs/look.md) sets how much. Doors: baked as at load (a leaf is not a collider; a portal end is faded to plain, and bounce never crosses a portal, each zone's grid having only its own lamps): a room behind a door takes no light from the hallway's bounce, but opening one does not change the fill either. Outdoor zones have no grid: the sky dominates there; sun and sky through a window are not sources in the bake (the probe still carries them). Cost: main thread about 40 ms (solids) + 0.4 s (surfaces), both while the GPU compiles; the workers about 1 s, done before the scene has compiled; the atlas is 1024 x 5382 RGBA half floats, about 44 MB (was 1024 x 2774, 22 MB). Something new and solid should be a collider (`solid`, `round`, `turned`) or it casts no ambient shade and bounces nothing.
- A lit material has 6 texture units to spend: 16 minus the 9 lamp shadow maps and the sun's. In a zone with fixtures or an ambient grid the atlas takes one more: a pool tile then uses all 6.

## Between places

- The picture's mood (exposure, fog, grade, bloom, shafts) depends on where you stand, not on when you crossed. At load `lights.js` finds the openings: for every two zones whose boxes touch, the stretches of the shared edge a body can walk straight through (no solid from 0.8 m before the edge to 0.8 m past it, no rise taller than a step). An edge behind a wall is no opening, so nothing blends through a wall.
- `weights(x, z, zone)`: within 2.5 m (BAND) of an opening the neighbour's share k rises linearly to one half on the edge (and falls off the same way along the wall beside it). Shares are counted as odds against the zone you stand in, k / (1 - k), and a zone behind one of that neighbour's openings gets the product of the two odds, so it counts only near the door into the neighbour (nothing through a wall). Since k from one side of an opening is 1 - k from the other, every zone's odds against every other agree whichever zone you count from: the shares do not jump when the zone you are in flips, even where three zones meet (the 1.4 m corner squares). The odds are then normalised.
- Passage warps are two 0.32 m strips meeting at the passage's middle (wider than a body moves in a frame). A self-check asserts that neither a strip nor where it lands is within BAND of an opening, so both twins show the passage's own mood at the move. A place a passage opens into needs its zone box to meet the passage's at the mouth, not reach into it (the glasshouse's once reached 0.4 m in, leaving too little room).
- `player.js mood()` eases each zone's share in time (about 0.12 s, for teleports) and mixes exposure on a log scale, the rest linearly. The fog is mixed as pictures, not numbers (`post.js` atmos, up to three zones): a thick black passage fog thins as a dark veil over the place's own colours, never as grey haze. The shared lamps use the same shares: a fixture's group loses its 8 m ranking handicap as you near its doorway, so a room's lamps come on before you step in and stay on while you look back. Pools and loose things are drawn (and ring shadows redrawn) from any zone in reach.
- A new place does nothing for this: give it a zone box that meets its neighbours' at the doorway (within 0.7 m). `tools/test.mjs` checks every opening can be walked through and that leaving a dark passage never washes grey; `tools/transitions.mjs` measures the rest.

## Pool pictures

Every pool in `water.js` lies on the same horizontal plane, `WY`. Its wide reflection and underwater cameras depend on that plane and the viewing camera, not the basin's position or floor. All pools are hidden during either capture. So there is one mirror/lens pair (one Reflector, one Refractor) for every pool: the first pool drawn in a scene render makes it, the others use it. Waves, floor tracing, plans and shoreline shading remain per pool.

Sizes. The pictures span `OVER` (1.45) times the screen each way. The underwater picture is shown unblurred, so it is `OVER` times the drawing buffer each way (`fit`, checked before each capture: resize, pixel ratio and render scale all follow), one texel per screen pixel: still water shows the floor as sharp as air at any screen size. It was a fixed 1280 square, 1.5x too coarse at 720p and 3x at 1440p. It has no MSAA, so sub-pixel geometry under water (a drain's 6 mm bars, at 720p) can stair-step; 4x costs about .3 ms at 1440p but its sample buffers would be about 370 MB there. The mirror stays a 1280 square: it is read with four taps .0007 of its width apart, a blur fixed in the picture, and 1.45x its pixels looked the same at 1440p. Where a bent ray ends outside the underwater picture (water right under a low eye), the colour comes from the plan, 80 px a metre; that band is visibly softer than the rest, a known limit.

The "made" flag resets on every non-override render from the main camera, including renders at an unchanged shader time; pictures are never carried across frames or camera changes. At or below the plane (within 1 mm) each pool captures into the pair again just before it draws (three skips a back-facing capture, and the water's back face is not drawn anyway). A pool at another height or orientation needs its own pair. `waterViews.reuse = false` makes every pool capture again, for diagnostic A/B pictures and timings.

Two things keep the pair cheap; `waterViews.cull = false` turns both off (`tools/pool-skip.mjs` compares against that).

- **Hidden pools make no pictures.** Each pool has an invisible proxy box (the water widened 1.5 m, raised 3 m), drawn first among the see-through things inside an occlusion query. The result arrives a frame late, so it is trusted only while fresh (4 frames, no `dev.reset` since), while the eye has moved under 15 cm since, while the eye is not within 40 cm of any solid (an edge that close sweeps metres of a far room per centimetre), and, unless the whole proxy was on screen, while the view has not turned or widened. Anything else counts as seen. The pair is skipped only when every pool drawn is judged hidden (corridor: both pools behind walls). Rendered in one task (`dev.step(n)`, `tools/shots.mjs`), no result is ever ready, so nothing is skipped and photos are as before. Waves, rings and balls are untouched: they follow `w.visible` (player.js), not this.
- **The pair is cut to what the water can sample** (render-target scissor, nothing drawn or cleared outside): the lens to the projected basin box (the bent ray ends inside the basin), the mirror to the projected box 9 m round the pool and below the surface, plus 3 pixels for the mirror's taps and filtering. Reflection now traces up to 9 m against the mirror's retained depth, stopping at the first captured surface; a miss uses the former 9 m lookup eased toward the picture edge. Every trace point remains inside the same scissor bound. This prevents a nearby wall being bypassed by the old fixed-distance lookup. If any corner of a box is behind the eye that picture is drawn whole; for the mirror that is nearly always, for the lens it cuts pool views to 30-45 %.

`node tools/pool-skip.mjs [--halls]` walks every opening next to a zone a pool is drawn from (running, jumping, spinning and flicking the view at pool doorways; `--halls` adds the centre lines) and compares every game frame with the same frame drawn with `cull` off, pixel for pixel, the pair filled with magenta between frames so a stale picture cannot pass for a fresh one. `tools/test.mjs` runs it for the night hall.

## Portals

Two openings joined as if they were one doorway, at any distance, height and turn about the vertical: you see through one into the place behind the other, with exact perspective, and walk through with no visible move. Used by the hotel's west door 34.8 and east door 38.4, which both open into the vestibule (`js/zones/hotel.js`), by its east door 24, which opens into the baths' entrance passage (declared in `js/zones/baths.js`, which finds that door in `build.js`'s `doors`, since places never import each other), by the terrace court's free-standing blue door, whose west face opens onto the sky stair hanging east of the lookout (`js/zones/terrace.js`; its east face is an ordinary doorway, and `portals.mjs`'s `same` cannot pass there, since around a free-standing door the two places differ by design), and by the dev yard's test pair (`js/zones/yard.js`).

**Adding a portal room:**

1. *Where it goes.* A room reached only through portals stands 200 m down (`const Y = -200`, every height of the room taken from it), at an x, z of its own, under the sea block (which covers ±3000 m at y −3): no eye anywhere else can see it, so `tools/pvs.mjs` lists it for nobody and gives it only what it sees itself (the vestibule's row is `[]`). Built at ground level it is seen standing in the distance and drawn from far zones (the vestibule was: listed by 5 zones, itself listing 8, and its far-side draw submitted all 8). Give its zone's `probe` and `env` the same Y.
2. *Its floor.* `floorH` is 0 wherever no flat says otherwise, so register the room's floor, reveals included: `flats.push([x1, z1, x2, z2, () => Y])`.
3. *The call* (in the place file, after both zones exist):

```js
portal([2.2, 0, 34.8, Math.PI / 2], [302.8, -200, 299.7, 0], 1, 2.24); // [x, y, z, ry] of each end, then width and height (both ends the same size)
```

   Each end is the middle of the opening's sill, on the plane where the two sides meet, and `ry` the way its front faces (as `rotateY(ry)` turns +z; the front is the side you stand on before going through). Put the plane at the back face of the wall the opening is cut in: the reveal and frame in front of it stay this side's own, and the far end's reveal continues it. Build each side as a real doorway (wall round the opening, a frame, no backing); what lies behind a plane on its own side never shows and never blocks.
4. Run `node tools/pvs.mjs` (rewrites `js/seen.js`), then the tests below, and look at it.

**What follows by itself:**

- *Seeing* (`js/portals.js`): after each scene draw from the main camera (colour, and the AO pass's normals and depth) every end in view (in front of it, its zone drawn, within 80 m) marks the stencil with a pocket behind its opening placed at the depth where each ray crosses the plane; there the depth is cleared and the scene drawn again from the eye taken through the link, same projection (so AO, fog, shafts and bloom see one picture), scissored to the opening, with the far end's zone layers, clipped at the far plane by the one clipping plane every program has (`clip` in `core.js`). Openings seen through openings are drawn the same way inside the picture, recursively (the gallery's two doors face across the hallway, so you see the gallery, the hallway, your own doorway, the gallery again...): each level's stencil value is one more, its scissor its rectangle clipped to its parent's, and three's view frustum for it is cropped to that rectangle with its near plane on the far end's plane, so a level submits only what can show in its opening. A nested opening ends (`LIM` in `portals.js`) when its rectangle's narrow side is under 6 px or it would be the 9th level (8 drawn: in the gallery loop at 2560×1440 the 8th opening is 13×30 px, about 78 m down). Where it ends, the opening is filled flat with the far zone's fog colour to the power 1.5 (times `LIM.fill`), measured against the mean of what further levels draw there, and in the AO pass's normals with the plane's normal: a dim warm distance, not a hole. `stats` in `portals.js` gives `levels` and each ended opening's pixel rectangle. A level's frustum also has a far plane: `tools/pvs.mjs` records for each end whose zone sees others how far the farthest surface seen through its opening lies, by direction (32 x 32 bins, `through` in `js/seen.js`), and the bins the rectangle's rays fall in bound the depth (a hallway end straight on: about 3 m, the opposite wall); while cropped it also tests each mesh's world box after its sphere (a merged chunk's sphere is 15 m across). A hallway level went from about 125 calls, 0.83 Mtri (the mall behind its walls) to about 50, 0.27. The resolve of the multisampled scene target three makes at the end of every `render()` (a full-size blit, 5 or 6 a level) is held during the portal pass and made once after it: the gallery seen across the hallway, 8 levels, scene pass 10.9 to 4.4 ms at 2560x1440 (3.9 with no portal drawn). `LIM.cull = false` turns the depth bound and box test off, for an A/B. A portal on screen costs what its far zone costs to draw (hotel west door: 3.3 ms a frame against 3.2 ms when it was a shut door, 1280×720); with none on screen it costs nothing.
- *Clipped in hardware*: `core.js` gives every material three's clip-distance extension (`gl_ClipDistance`, switched on with `gl.enable`, which three never does) and drops three's `discard` test, which costs every shader its early depth test. Without `WEBGL_clip_cull_distance` three's discard stays: correct, slower. The MSAA target's stencil is never resolved (`resolveStencilBuffer: false` in `post.js`: on D3D resolving it tripled the scene pass in every view).
- *Walking* (`js/player.js`): the centre crossing the plane inside the opening (eye within its height) takes position, frame start, the rest of the move, speed, facing and height through, inside the 10 cm sub-steps. A body straddling the plane collides with this side's solids not wholly behind it and the far side's not wholly behind the far end. The floor-step look-ahead reads the floor through the portal when its step crosses the opening (`floorVia`). Noclip goes through too.
- *Zone, mood and lamps*: a body in a portal end's reveal (`reveal()` in `build.js`: the 1 m strip in front of the opening) is in that end's zone whatever box holds it (the hotel's west-door reveal lies in the mall's box: the mood used to flip to the mall for two frames), so `weights()` always measures from the right space. Each pair is an opening between its two zones, so the mood blends exactly as at a doorway; a lamp fixture in front of a far end ranks by its distance from your place taken through (through up to three open portals in a row, each within 30 m: the lift lobby seen from the vestibule across the hallway gets its lamp).
- *Culling*: the far side draws what its end's zone sees (`js/seen.js`), inside its opening's rectangle, in front of the far end's plane and no deeper than its `through` table allows (above); an eye in a portal's reveal also draws that end's zone. `tools/pvs.mjs` shuts every opening; run it again (it writes both) after changing what a portal end looks onto.

*Doors* (`door(..., { openable: true })` in `props/metalwork.js`, swung by `stepDoors` in `player.js`): one door, double-acting (a centre pivot and floor spring), whose leaf swings away from whoever presses E, so E then W always walks you through. Put the end's plane through the frame's middle (the hallway's are at 2.55 and 5.05, the frame centres) and build the door's copy at the linked place: `const [a] = portal(A, B, w, h, d); a.to.door = door(z, ...via(a, x, y, zc, ry), { ...same options, copy: d })` (`via()` in `build.js` takes a pose through). The copy has its own frame and leaf meshes and solids; `d.set(f, dir)` moves both. *A moving thing straddling a portal*: each copy's frame is cut at the plane by the drawing as any wall is (the far one by the clip, the near one by the pocket), but the leaf is lit face by face from the copy on the side each face faces, whichever side the eye is on, so it is the same leaf one centimetre either side of the plane at any angle: `door()` splits each leaf material into three meshes by `userData.face` (1 the +z face and what stands on it, -1 the -z face, 0 the edges), and right after its opening's picture (before that picture is marked taken) `portals.js` draws this side's copy whole over it, then the far copy's faces toward the far room (an edge group goes with the side the leaf stands on; shut, with the original) from the eye taken through the link, unclipped, polygon-offset so they win over this side's copy of the same faces. A door's draw reaches only this level's own pixels and its own opening's picture (and those inside it), never another opening's: a free-standing door's far end seen from the near side under open sky (nothing to hide it by depth) was drawn hanging in the near end's picture. The far copy is hidden in the far draw. Lamps never cross a portal, so a face lit by the room it faces is the nearest thing to light through the doorway. A portal with no turn can do better: give the far copy and the floor just past it `shade` (the link's offset, docs/materials.md) and they are lit exactly as the near end, shadows, ambient and reflections included (the terrace court door: the arcade's stripes run on across the sky stair's flat top and stop at its first riser). The sun's shadow map is fixed per sky (`at`/`r` in `sky.js`), not following the player, so the borrowed lookup must land inside it. Both copies cast shadows in their own space (the shadow cache's loose list, `physics.js`); a body straddling the plane collides with both. E opens the door looked at (within 1.6 m of its leaf's middle, facing it): latch 0.1 s, then a 0.8 s eased swing; a step that would put either copy of the leaf into the body is not taken, so it stops against you; closing goes back the way it came. While a door is shut (`live(e)` false) the leaf fills the opening from both sides, so its portal is not drawn, adds nothing to the mood or the sound (an opening's share scales with `gap(e)`, full at a quarter open), and its far side's lamps are ranked only within 3 m of it, so they are settled before the gap first shows. `tools/portals.mjs` checks the pictures (same, sweep), the far draw's clip (clip: a card behind the far plane must not show) and recursion (deep: the gallery from across the hallway draws at least three levels and ends only for size), from the suite's `move` area.

**Limits:**

- *A pool seen through a portal* is not drawn (`portals.js` hides the ponds in the far picture). The pools' mirror and under-water pictures are made for the main camera; to show one, `pass()` would make them again from the portal camera for each pond its far zone sees (at 1280×720 about 2.4 ms mirror + 1 ms under per pool, when one is on screen through a portal) and then draw the ponds inside the stencil. Needed before a door leads into a pool hall.
- *A different sky either side*: the sun, its one shadow map (covering the sky's `at`/`r` ground), and the sky dome are this side's; the shafts follow each ray of the nearest opening drawn through into the far space (`post.js` atmos, `stats.top` in `portals.js`), so a turned portal under the sun shows the far side's shafts and glow. Openings past the nearest keep this side's shafts. Two zones of the same sky (`day`: the hotel and the pool hall) need nothing; a different sky needs `setSky` around the far draw and a second sun shadow render a frame (its texture unit can be reused; the render is the cost). A portal room 200 m down is outside every sun's shadow ground: give it a ceiling and no sky of its own.
- Both ends the same size (asserted in `merge()`).
- Loose balls and rings do not go through (a ball meets the far side's solids only in its own space). See-through things on this side in front of an opening are drawn under the far picture. A custom `ShaderMaterial` needs `clipping: true` to be clipped at the far plane. The cube-map bakes do not see through portals.

**For sound** (the spatial sound system): `build.js` exports `ends` (each with `p`, `n`, `w`, `h`, `to`, `zone`), `side(e, x, z)` and `across(e, x, z)` (a point against an opening), `carry(e, x, z)` (a point taken through `e`; height: add `e.to.p.y - e.p.y`), `spin(e, x, z)` (a direction taken through) and `reveal(e, x, z)`. The ends in reach of the player at (x, z) are `ends.filter(e => { const d = side(e, x, z); return d >= 0 && d < 30; })` (as `lights.js` ranks lamps); a sound at q on the far side of such an end `e` is heard as if at `carry(e.to, q)`, coming through the opening's middle `e.p`.

**Testing:** `node tools/portals.mjs [--only i,j] [--modes walk,run,hop,diag,back,same,sweep]` walks every end both ways (walking, running, hopping at 7.2 m/s in 0.05 s frames, diagonally, backing out mid-plane) and checks where you end (a walk pushed off its line by a solid away from the plane, an open leaf say, is only reported; pushed off while straddling the plane fails), that crossing is no jump of state (each crossing frame and the three after it drawn again with the state of the frame before, against the same on the three before it), that the picture with the eye in the plane is the same from both sides, and that moving through the plane in 2 cm steps shows no seam. The suite runs ends 0 and 1 (walk, hop, same, sweep) in `move`. ## Sound

Everything is synthesised (no files, no requests). `js/music.js` is the music; `js/sound.js` the effects, built on `js/voices.js`, which writes every one-shot (footsteps, doors, splashes, drips, knocks, crickets, the reverbs' impulses) sample by sample. `js/audio.js` starts both on the first click and calls the effects once a frame from `player.js`.

- **The interface**, once the sound has started (`sfx` from `js/audio.js`, `null` before the first click):
  - `sfx.play(name, x, y, z, { gain, rate })` plays a one-shot there: `door.wood.open`, `door.wood.close`, `door.metal.open`, `door.metal.close`, `splash.small`, `splash`, `splash.big`, `drip`, `knock.ball`, `knock.pit`, `knock.ring`, `knock.big`, `cricket`, `step.<surface>`. Returns false when it was dropped (the voice cap).
  - `const h = sfx.loop(name, x, y, z, level)` starts a looping sound there (`hum`, `vent`, `lap`, `washer`, `dryer`, `surf`, `roll`); `h.move(x, y, z)`, `h.stop()`.
  - e.g. a door that starts to swing: `sfx?.play('door.wood.open', 3.8, 1, 36)`; a fan in a new room: `const fan = sfx?.loop('vent', x, 2.6, z, .5)`.
- **Floors.** `merge()` in `js/build.js` keeps the top of every slab, step and box by its material on a 2 m grid (`floorAt(x, z, feet)`); `surfaceOf` in `js/sound.js` maps each library material to a surface: `carpet`, `rubber`, `tile`, `wetTile` (tile in a pool room), `concrete`, `stone`, `wood`, `metal`, `grass`, `gravel`, plus `pit` (down among the soft play's balls), `shallow` and `wade` (by how deep you stand in a pool). A material outside the library says what it is with `mat.userData.surface = 'wood'`; where nothing in the index is under the feet (a hill, a curved floor) the zone's `step` is used. A new library material that is walked on gets a line in `surfaceOf`.
- **A place's air**: `amb: { ... }` in its zone definition, each a level (about 0 to 1): `hum` (each lit fixture hums from where it hangs, the four nearest; and a faint room hum), `vent` and `ventF` (its band, Hz), `water`, `drips`, `wind`, `grass` (both move with one shared gust that never repeats), `traffic`, `crickets`, `rumble` (and now and then a far train), `machines: [[x, y, z, 'washer' | 'dryer'], ...]`, `sea: [x1, z1, x2, z2, y]` (the surf heard from the nearest point of that line) and `seaLv`, `verb: [dead, room, hall]` to override the reverb. Without `amb` a zone gets its `hum`, `water` and `air` numbers, a vent if it has an `env` box, and a reverb from its `wet` (how much) and its `env` box (how big; carpet deadens it; outdoors next to none).
- **Through doorways.** Every zone has a bus: what is in it is heard through its lowpass. Your zone and those within reach of a doorway (`weights()`) are clear in proportion to their share; a zone one opening away is heard faintly and muffled, more so the farther you are from that opening; anything else is silent. The room tone of each zone fades by the square root of its share, so a doorway crossing holds its loudness. Your own footsteps take the reverb of where you stand, blended the same way.
- **Space.** Placed sounds are HRTF panned with inverse distance roll-off, and a lowpass closes from 18 kHz in front to 4.5 kHz behind.
- **Caps.** 14 placed one-shots, 6 footsteps, 4 fixture hums, 3 pool edges, one surf line per place, one rolling ball, one wading swirl; knocks are rate-limited (three a frame, 30-70 ms apart per kind). A zone's bed is made when it becomes audible and stopped 3 s after it is not.
- **Levels.** Effects sit under the music (`fx` gain 0.55 into the master, before the compressor). Footstep surfaces carry a `loud` in `js/voices.js`. Measure with `node tools/sound.mjs` (docs/testing.md).
